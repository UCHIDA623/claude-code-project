Canva vs. Figma: Which Design Tool Fits Your Team?

Published 2026-09-18 · 5 min read

Canva and Figma are both hugely popular design tools, but they were built for different purposes. Canva focuses on fast, template-driven graphic design for everyday content, while Figma is built for precise interface design and collaborative product workflows. This article compares the two products on pricing, features, ease of use, and integrations so you can decide which one fits your team.

What Is Canva?

Canva is a drag-and-drop design tool aimed at non-designers who need to create social media graphics, presentations, marketing materials, and documents quickly. Its huge library of templates, stock photos, and fonts lets anyone produce polished visuals in minutes, without needing prior design training.

What Is Figma?

Figma is a professional interface design and prototyping tool used primarily by product designers and UX teams to design websites, apps, and design systems. It is built around vector editing, reusable components, and real-time multiplayer collaboration, making it the de facto standard for modern product design teams.

Pricing

Both tools offer free plans, with paid tiers that scale by number of seats or editors.

Plan Canva Figma
Free Core templates and assets 3 editable files, unlimited viewers
Pro/Standard ~$13/user/month (Teams) ~$16/editor/month (Figma Design)
Enterprise Custom pricing Custom pricing

Canva's free tier is generous for casual, everyday design work, while Figma's free tier is more limited but still practical for small design projects and prototypes.

Features and Ease of Use

Canva's template-first approach means most users can produce a finished graphic within minutes, with almost no learning curve. However, it lacks the precision tools — pixel-level control, auto layout, and component variants — that professional product design requires. Figma offers that precision along with powerful prototyping and design-system features, but it has a steeper learning curve and is overkill for someone who just needs a quick social media post.

Integrations

Canva integrates well with social platforms, Google Drive, and content calendars, supporting a content-creation workflow end to end. Figma integrates deeply with developer and product tools such as Jira, Slack, and code-export plugins, and its API enables design-to-code handoff, which is central to product development teams' workflows.

Verdict

Choose Canva if your team needs to produce marketing graphics, social posts, and presentations quickly without specialized design skills. Choose Figma if you are designing digital products and need precise, collaborative interface design with a shared design system. Neither tool is universally "better" — the right choice depends on whether your priority is speed and accessibility or precision and product workflows.
