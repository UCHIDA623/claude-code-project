Zapier vs. Make (Integromat): Which Automation Tool Fits Your Team?

Published 2026-09-12 · 6 min read

In short, Zapier is the better fit for teams automating workflows for the first time, or anyone who wants to connect apps quickly with minimal setup. Make (formerly Integromat) is the better fit for teams that need complex branching logic or want to run advanced automations while keeping costs down. Zapier's strengths are its huge library of app integrations and a simple, guided interface, though it comes at a higher price. Make's visual flow editor makes it easier to build complex workflows and often costs less for the same volume of work, but it has a steeper learning curve than Zapier. Below, we break down pricing, ease of use, and the difference in integration counts, then close with use-case-based recommendations.

What Is Zapier?

Zapier is a cloud automation service that connects web applications through "Zaps" — combinations of a trigger and one or more actions — to eliminate manual, repetitive work. Its no-code, wizard-style interface lets even non-technical users build an integration in minutes. As one of the pioneers of workflow automation, Zapier is also the industry leader when it comes to the sheer number of supported apps.

What Is Make (Integromat)?

Make is a visual automation platform that rebranded from "Integromat" in 2022. Its defining feature is a flowchart-style canvas where users connect modules to build branching logic, loops, and error handling visually. Make offers more configuration freedom than Zapier and can bundle multiple operations into a single run, which often makes it the more cost-effective option for complex workflows.

Pricing

Both tools offer free plans, but they bill differently. Zapier meters usage in "tasks" (each completed action), while Make meters usage in "operations" (the processing units consumed per scenario run) — a distinction worth understanding before you compare the numbers directly.

Entry tier ~$19.99/month (from 750 tasks) ~$9/month (from 10,000 operations)

For comparable workloads, Make tends to be the cheaper option. Because Zapier consumes a task for every step in a Zap while Make counts by operation, Make's cost advantage grows with workflow complexity. That said, many of Zapier's more advanced features (multi-step Zaps, conditional logic) are locked behind higher tiers, so if your needs are simple, its free or entry-level plan is often perfectly workable.

Ease of Use

Zapier follows a straightforward wizard: pick a trigger, pick an action, configure it. That linear flow means people with no prior automation experience can set up a working Zap without getting lost. Its documentation and template library are extensive, so an example for almost any use case is usually just a search away, keeping the learning curve very shallow.

Make, by contrast, uses a visual canvas where users drag and connect modules by hand. Features like routers and aggregators let you express branching logic that would be difficult to replicate in Zapier. That flexibility comes at a cost: users unfamiliar with flowchart-style or programming-adjacent thinking face a steeper initial learning curve before they can build confidently.

Integrations

Zapier supports more than 7,000 apps, putting it well ahead of the rest of the automation market. Its catalog covers not just major SaaS products but also niche business tools and smaller regional services, so it's rare to find an app you want to connect that isn't already supported.

Make supports more than 2,000 apps — fewer than Zapier — but it compensates with generic HTTP/API modules and webhook support that let technically capable users connect to virtually any service, even without an official connector. The real trade-off is breadth of pre-built integrations versus the freedom to build custom connections for anything with an API.

Which Should You Choose? (By Use Case)

Choose Zapier if: you're new to automation and want to start with simple notification or data-transfer workflows; the apps you need to connect are niche and breadth of support matters most; or non-technical team members will be building and maintaining the Zaps themselves.

Choose Make if: you need to automate complex workflows involving conditional branching or loops; a single scenario handles a high volume of operations and you want to keep costs down; or your team has members comfortable with APIs and data structures who can design and maintain more flexible workflows.

Using both is also an option: many companies build simple notification-style automations quickly in Zapier while reserving Make for more complex processes involving core-system integrations or data transformation.

Verdict

Zapier and Make are both excellent automation tools, but they serve different needs. Choose Zapier if you want a huge library of integrations and a simple interface that lets anyone start automating right away. Choose Make if you want the freedom of a visual flow editor and better cost efficiency at higher volumes. Neither tool is universally "better" — the right choice depends on how complex your workflows are, your team's technical skill set, and your budget. Try both on their free plans first to see which one fits your actual use case.
