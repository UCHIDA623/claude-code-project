Notion vs. Confluence: Which Knowledge Base Tool Fits Your Team?

Published 2026-09-11 · 5 min read

Notion and Confluence are two of the most popular tools teams use to document processes, manage projects, and build an internal knowledge base. Both promise to be the single source of truth for your organization, but they take very different approaches. This article compares the two products on pricing, features, ease of use, and integrations so you can decide which one fits your team.

What Is Notion?

Notion is an all-in-one workspace that combines notes, wikis, databases, and light project management into a single flexible canvas. Instead of rigid page structures, Notion gives users building blocks — text, tables, kanban boards, calendars — that can be arranged freely. This flexibility makes it popular with startups and small teams that want one tool for many jobs. If project management is your primary need rather than documentation, see our Asana vs. Monday.com comparison.

What Is Confluence?

Confluence, made by Atlassian, is a purpose-built team documentation and wiki platform. It is tightly integrated with Jira, which makes it a natural choice for software teams that already track issues and sprints in the Atlassian ecosystem. Confluence favors structured spaces, page hierarchies, and permission controls suited to larger organizations.

Notion vs. Confluence Pricing Comparison

Both tools offer free plans for small teams, with paid tiers that scale by number of users.

Plan Notion Confluence
Free Unlimited blocks for individuals Up to 10 users
Standard/Plus ~$10/user/month ~$6/user/month
Enterprise Custom pricing Custom pricing

Confluence tends to be cheaper per seat at mid-size scale, while Notion's pricing is simpler with fewer tiers to navigate.

Features and Ease of Use

Notion's drag-and-drop editor is intuitive and requires almost no training, which is why non-technical teams adopt it quickly. However, that same flexibility can lead to messy, inconsistent documentation as a workspace grows without clear conventions. Confluence has a steeper learning curve but enforces more structure through templates, spaces, and page trees, which helps larger teams keep documentation organized and easy to navigate over time.

Notion vs. Confluence: Integrations and Jira Compatibility

Confluence's biggest strength is its native integration with Jira and the broader Atlassian suite, letting teams link requirements, tickets, and documentation seamlessly. Notion offers a growing library of integrations (Slack, GitHub, Google Drive, and more) and a public API, but it is not as deeply woven into any single ecosystem, making it more of a generalist tool. Notion can also be connected to hundreds of other apps through automation platforms — see our Zapier vs. Make comparison for details.

Notion vs. Confluence: Which Should You Choose?

Best for Startups and Small Teams

Choose Notion if you want a flexible, easy-to-learn workspace that can handle notes, docs, and light project management in one place — ideal for startups and small teams.

Best for Jira Users and Larger Organizations

Choose Confluence if your team already lives in Jira and needs structured, permission-controlled documentation that scales with a larger organization.

Neither tool is universally "better" — the right choice depends on your team's size, workflow, and existing tool stack.
